How training works

The dog learns the system, and the owner learns how to maintain it. Brandi builds the foundation with your dog, brings you into the work in stages, and supports the transition for 30 days after your dog goes home.

The five stages

  1. Evaluate
  2. Establish
  3. Integrate
  4. Transfer
  5. Support

Who works with the dog, stage by stage

  1. Evaluate

    Working with the dog: Brandi, with your history and goals

    The Consultation & Evaluation is used to understand your dog and build the training plan. It may cover your dog's history, current behavior, temperament, triggers, the commands it already knows, previous training, handling, and your concerns and goals.

    When appropriate, it can include interaction with other dogs or with people.

    After you send an inquiry, Brandi replies to arrange the next step.

    A signed service agreement is required before training or boarding begins.

    Request a consultation

  2. Establish

    Working with the dog: Brandi

    Brandi works primarily with the dog first, building the foundation, communication, expectations, structure, and responses.

    When needed, this can include changing command words or setting up a different communication system.

  3. Integrate

    Working with the dog: Brandi and you, with your part growing

    Once the foundation is established, you are brought into the training while your dog is still in the program.

  4. Transfer

    Working with the dog: you, with Brandi

    Your part grows as the program goes on. Brandi teaches you the commands, timing, handling, reinforcement, corrections when appropriate, equipment use, boundaries, how to respond to your dog's specific behaviors, and how to maintain the system at home.

    Toward the end of the program, you handle your dog while Brandi observes and coaches. The program runs approximately 14 days in total, adjusted to the individual dog.

  5. Support

    Working with the dog: you, at home

    Support continues for 30 days after your dog returns home. It is used to see how the training carries into your home, correct misunderstandings, troubleshoot problems, reinforce handling, and help you and your dog settle into the new routine. Follow-up terms
